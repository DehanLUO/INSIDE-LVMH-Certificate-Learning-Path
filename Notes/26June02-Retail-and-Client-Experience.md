### Luxury Retail today & tomorrow

Get a first glimpse of **Retail and Client Experience** in the luxury industry.

#### Reinventing luxury Retail: where digital innovation meets human connection

**Sandrine Crener**, Leadership Learning Director at **LVMH** and Instructor at **Harvard University**, explains how Retail in the luxury industry is undergoing a profound transformation.

From digital acceleration to evolving consumer values, the world of luxury retail is being redefined. In this article, we explore how brands are adapting to new realities and preparing for the client experience of tomorrow.

##### The evolution of luxury

The nature of business is to evolve, and luxury retail is no exception. Over the past decade, the industry has shifted from a traditional model, centred around a few exclusive boutiques in select global cities, to an omnichannel reality where digital and physical experiences must seamlessly coexist. This transition is driven by the emergence of new client segments (Millennials, Gen Z, and affluent consumers from emerging markets) and their expectations for inclusivity, transparency, and accessibility.

Popular e-commerce marketplaces, while not direct competitors, have nonetheless transformed consumer expectations when it comes to convenience and service. Luxury must respond by matching these standards while preserving the magic and exclusivity that define it.

Building on this transformation, the integration of digital platforms and data-centric operations is fundamentally reshaping luxury retail. Brands now have the opportunity to harness advanced analytics and AI to deliver precision in personalization, optimize supply chains, and reimagine their engagement strategies. As digital-native consumers set the pace, agility and algorithmic decision-making are becoming core capabilities for luxury players.

Similarly, ultra-fast fashion platforms are shifting norms around speed, price, and trend access, particularly for Gen Z. They have redefined what it means to be fast: launching thousands of new styles weekly, responding to viral social media content in real time, and gamifying the shopping experience to maximize engagement. These platforms use real-time data and consumer feedback loops to enable rapid micro-targeting and trend adaptation, effectively turning client behaviour into a design and production input. This data-driven agility represents a new operational paradigm that challenges traditional luxury timelines and marketing models.

> Today’s consumers are more educated, connected, and values-driven. They seek meaningful consumption aligned with their identities and beliefs. Brands must respond by becoming more authentic, transparent, and emotionally resonant.

##### What makes luxury Retail unique?

Luxury is uniquely positioned to deliver deeply human experiences. The pandemic highlighted a fundamental need for connection, which luxury retail can fulfil through personalized, relational interactions. Brands that excel at building emotional connections, both online and offline, will benefit from greater loyalty, advocacy, and long-term growth.

Digital tools now enable more immersive and participatory experiences. Consumers can co-create, voice preferences, and engage actively with brands. At the same time, companies can use data to anticipate behaviours, optimize supply chains, and personalize service. However, this comes with a responsibility: to use data ethically and non-intrusively (McKinsey & Company, 2023). Digital technologies, especially AI, are not just tools for automation; they are engines for new forms of client intimacy. When deployed responsibly, they enable continuous learning loops between brands and consumers, creating dynamic ecosystems of engagement and trust.

Brands face increasing scrutiny regarding data privacy, transparency in AI-driven personalization, and sustainability. Ethical and transparent use of consumer data is not just good practice; it is becoming a competitive necessity. Consumers increasingly demand accountability and clear standards from brands.

##### Adapting to new consumer expectations

Today’s luxury consumers expect personalization at scale, a complex challenge that calls for operational excellence and agility. They evaluate brands not only on product quality but on how they treat their employees, their environmental impact, and their social values. Tomorrow’s luxury will be more inclusive, more community-focused, and more accountable. This shift benefits both consumers and brands, opening access to broader, more diverse audiences (BCG x Altagamma, 2023).

Brands must respond differently across markets, such as digital-first, value-driven strategies in Asia or sustainability-focused campaigns in Europe, to resonate authentically with local consumer expectations. Embracing regional nuances is vital for maintaining relevance. Moreover, in a world where traditional boundaries between industries blur, luxury brands must learn to operate like digital platforms: open, adaptive, and data-driven.

##### Looking Ahead: The Next 10 Years

Predicting the future requires humility. But we can expect continued blurring between physical and digital realms. Just as music evolved from physical media to streaming, luxury may see its own products and experiences dematerialized, from NFTs (Non-Fungible Tokens) to digital fashion to virtual brand ecosystems.

Data will remain a strategic asset: the more a brand understands its clients, the more it can deliver bespoke experiences. Yet the true differentiator will be emotional intelligence: how technology supports, not replaces, the human dimension.

We might soon visit virtual flagship stores in immersive metaverses or engage with AI-driven personal stylists that anticipate our needs with remarkable accuracy. This evolution will redefine luxury engagement, combining technological innovation with genuine human experiences. The companies that master this fusion of technology and humanity will redefine competitive advantage, not just by what they sell, but by how intelligently and empathetically they serve.

As a final thought: retail is no longer just a transactional space. It is an experiential platform, a gateway to the full brand universe. From flagship stores to luxury hotels and restaurants (e.g., Louis Vuitton, Cheval Blanc, Bvlgari), retail becomes a powerful stage for storytelling, immersion, and lasting emotional impact (Bain & Company, 2025). Increasingly, AI-powered shopping agents are also entering the landscape, offering tailored product suggestions, streamlining purchasing journeys, and enhancing online interactions with a level of personalization once reserved for in-store experiences. These intelligent systems mark a significant step toward merging convenience with luxury-level service.

### A cornerstone of luxury

#### Retail seen as a new purpose in the brand ecosystem

**Anne Michaut**, Marketing Professor at **HEC Paris**, questions the purpose of **Retail** today within the brand ecosystem.

##### What is the purpose of Retail today?

The retail landscape is evolving from a traditional omnichannel approach, covering wholesale, e-commerce, social media, and more, toward a fully converged model. In this new model, all channels work together with the shared goal of connecting with clients and being constantly available to them, requiring retail to redefine its role and purpose through continuous and complementary interaction with the digital world.

This context has shown the critical role Retail plays when it comes **to creating and fostering connections with clients**.

Consequently, the **purpose** of retail is to provide a unique opportunity to:

1. **Magnify the product** and convey its unique performance. In luxury, the product is precious and is to be treated as such. Its performance is celebrated.

2. **Celebrate the brand** with a **scenario** at a global level, which is then reinterpreted in each store with a unique and specific version of this scenario. This leads to creating the entertaining variety that customers are seeking. Why is this essential?

   Because brands need to both create an overall **coherence** for their clients that increasingly visit their stores in multiple countries and cities, and because of **variety**, which is essential in providing clients with the opportunity to have a differentiated experience within the brand.

3. **Nurture client emotion** and, for this, require **identifying** characteristics such as **senses** that will particularly ignite relevant emotions for the brand.

The experience in stores can generate a unique direct emotional relationship by moving from a product (often functional) focus in the store to an experience (often emotional) focus.

In conclusion, the purpose of Retail today has to be considered in a very **holistic manner** in relationship with client journeys. From creating awareness to interest and desire, the Retail environment may have multiple functions for the brand, beyond the **act of purchase**, including in the post-purchase phase of the client journey.

#### Moments and decision making: the customer journey

Understanding the decision-making path is crucial for creating meaningful customer experiences. **Anne Michaut**, Marketing Professor at **HEC Paris**, outlines the five distinct stages of the customer journey, from awareness to final purchase.

I am Anne Michaut, Marketing Professor at HEC Paris, and we are going to talk about experiences, but more specifically about customer journeys and experiences, which means that we are going to look at the objectives of these experiences.

In our exploration of brand experiences, we turn our attention to a critical aspect of customer engagement: the decision-making path. Understanding how customers move from initial awareness to final purchase and beyond is crucial for creating meaningful brand experiences. The decision-making journey can be understood through five distinct steps.

##### Awareness

The first stage is about creating awareness that extends beyond traditional target customers. This is where brands begin to establish their presence in the market. Press coverage, social media campaigns, and strategic events play a pivotal role in introducing the brand to a broader audience. The goal is not just to be seen, but to be *noticed* in a way that sparks *curiosity* and *initial interest*. For instance, luxury Maisons are hosting engaging exhibitions open to all and inviting broad audiences to understand not just their products, but their creative processes and core values, as well.

##### Interest

Once awareness is established, the focus shifts to generating genuine interest among potential customers. Websites become more than just digital storefronts. They transform into immersive experiences. User-generated content adds authenticity, allowing potential customers to see real experiences and connections with the brand. Events, of course, continue to play a crucial role, offering deeper insights and more engaging interactions that move beyond simple awareness.

##### Desire

At this stage, the brand must ignite a deeper emotional connection with authentic narratives. One-to-one interactions become particularly powerful, allowing brands to create personalized experiences that transform interest into genuine desire. The brand moves from being something known to something coveted. Igniting desire requires a strategic segmentation of customers to target their specific aspirations in their experiences with the brand. Take music speakers for instance: some customers are drawn to technological innovation itself, while others seek emotional connection through music. These distinct customer profiles require fundamentally different approaches to igniting desire, recognizing that its origin varies across consumer segments.

##### Action

The action stage is where interest turns into purchase. More than a transaction, this moment is *a pivotal opportunity* to deepen *brand love* and forge *emotional connection*. Luxury brands elevate this experience through personalized touches. Careful wrapping or bespoke services contribute to a narrative of exclusivity and emotional investment. Buying a car, a watch or a handbag becomes a memorable, almost ritualistic experience rather than just an acquisition. Each detail reinforces exclusivity, making the customer feel part of the brand's world, turning customers into passionate brand advocates. At this stage, brands must also build confidence in the decision. Seamless guidance, from guided pre-selection to AI-powered recommendations, helps customers make informed choices. The goal is to amplify desire while reducing uncertainty, ensuring the product feels like an aspirational, must-have object. The purchase moment, therefore, is more than a sale; it is a pivotal brand touchpoint that transforms customers into passionate advocates. The purchase moment becomes a powerful touchpoint where brands can communicate their values, creativity, and emotional essence, transforming a commercial interaction into a deeply personal connection.

##### Post-Action

The journey does not end with the purchase; it evolves into deeper engagement and loyalty. **Post-purchase experiences** should enhance ownership pride and strengthen emotional ties with the brand. Luxury brands achieve this through exclusive events, workshops and curated experiences. Car brands invite customers to track days. Leather goods Maisons offer care and restoration workshops. These activities reinforce craftsmanship, exclusivity and belonging, as well as emotional attachment to their objects. *Private previews*, *VIP gatherings*, and *masterclasses* keep customers *engaged*, turning *one-time buyers* into lifelong brand ambassadors.

A seamless brand experience across all touchpoints - retail, digital, social - ensures **relevance and continuity**. Brands must **maintain excellence at every interaction** while also driving value. The **decision-making** path is not linear but **dynamic**, requiring brands to create meaningful, interconnected experiences at every stage. Those that **succeed** turn transactions into **enduring relationships**.

### Experiences in Retail

#### Experiences as a key lever to engage clients

According to **Sonja Prokopec**, Professor of luxury brand management at ESSEC Business School, consumers used to let brands do the driving. Each brand had a story to tell, and by choosing one over another, consumers were effectively buying into one brand’s narrative and letting it speak on their behalf. A purchase was a form of self-expression. One might say you were what you bought.

This shift, as consumers spend less money on tangible things and more on experiences, represents a **critical challenge for luxury brands**. Their consumers are expecting more than just the best quality, so luxury brands will need to give them the experience they are looking for, whether online or in stores. In other words, what we call ‘**experiential marketing**’ has become a must.

So here are the three keys to engaging today’s luxury consumers through experiential marketing.

##### Speak to their hearts: delight them!

Historically, travel and luxury experiences were often superficial, focusing on brief visits to attractions or standardized five-star hotels. Shopping abroad meant encountering familiar European boutiques globally. However, millennials are now driving significant changes, seeking **authentic, in-depth local experiences off the beaten path**. Before the pandemic, they favoured peer-to-peer platforms to immerse themselves in local culture, food, and artisan goods, a trend further accelerated by recent events.

> You do not just go there to shop; you go to be immersed in an experience.

Art also offers a powerful axis through which to speak to the hearts of consumers, as it **resonates emotionally and sparks curiosity** around the artistic process. Many brands have established dedicated museums, such as the Louis Vuitton Foundation in Paris, integrating art into their brand storytelling. The Christian Dior Exhibit at the Musée des Arts Décoratifs similarly elevates the founder's artistic legacy. Chaumet's "Joséphine et Napoléon" exhibition also showcased 150 pieces, immersing visitors in the Maison's history.

**But the luxury-art union also unravels through other forms of collaboration and creation** that go beyond museum exhibitions. Dior has contributed its creations to ballet, such as those for the Philip Glass-composed ballet in Rome, while Guerlain's "Femmes en regard" solidarity project used photography exhibitions to amplify artists' voices. These are all examples that reinforce the idea that art and luxury go hand-in-hand.

##### Speak to their minds: entertain them!

**Storytelling** is one of the best ways to engage consumers. Today’s consumers are hungry to learn about the history of each brand, their creative processes, and their artistic inspirations. They want to be engaged on an intellectual level but also **be entertained, both in-stores and online**. This growing desire translates to a burgeoning “content craze”. Content is indeed key; however, it also needs to be credible, authentic, unique, and fun to fully involve clients. Storytelling takes consumers on a journey of discovery, even if that journey is only a few seconds long.

**Moreover, immersive experiences can be created through numerous tools**. Christian Dior, for example, has experimented with the presentation of Haute Couture by creating short films that have the ability to engage the viewer and take them into another dimension. With ‘Le Mythe Dior’ (2020) or ‘Le Château du Tarot’ (2021), the clothes from the catwalks blend into a different universe, where the scenery absorbs and immerses the viewer completely, who is fully transported into the world of the Maison.

Closely aligned with the theme of entertainment, the **phenomenon of gamification** is rapidly expanding within a market growing by 20% annually. LVMH has responded promptly to this trend with the alliance between Louis Vuitton and Riot Games, where they partnered in the League of Legends game with branded collections for the characters. This is branded entertainment for luxury consumers – a segment of consumers constantly looking for a new and fresh kind of experience.

##### Speak to their senses: get them immersed!

**Engaging today's luxury consumers means stimulating all five senses** by inviting them to see intricate detailing and feel fabric textures during a shopping experience. Scents and sounds are equally, if not more, important. Unlike other senses, scent triggers an automatic, subconscious response. A hint of Dior J'adore perfume can evoke the entire Dior brand identity, especially when paired with a visual cue like a logo or an iconic handbag.

Guerlain's Mindscent, a fragrance finder powered by emotion sensors, is a prime example of innovation in this area. Through neuronal headsets and visual interfaces, clients engage in a three-minute interactive experience, guided by a beauty consultant, to discover the fragrance that brings them the most positive emotional reaction and best matches their personality.

Combining several senses creates a 360° stimulation, building associations that **amplify the consumer's experience**. Brands like Fendi have experimented with permanent cafes and pop-up spaces, offering brand-inspired cappuccinos and cocktails. In an experiential world, a dinner or coffee becomes an expression of the self.

Every aspect of the consumer's experience must be consistent, complementary, and streamlined. Luxury retailers should reintroduce spontaneity into retail environments, both online and offline, by inviting consumers on a journey of discovery.

#### Customer engagement strategies through immersive phygital experiences

**Emanuela Prandelli**, LVMH Associate Professor at **Bocconi University**, shares with us the keys for luxury brands to engage with clients: rethink physical stores and deliver highly involving **phygital experiences** to clients.

Luxury stores all over the world have over time become more similar to each other, for quite a long while, offering the same brands, regardless of the local context. Hosting the same collection to satisfy a global customer and setting up identical shop windows and store layouts around the world has been considered a plus to build a very homogeneous image in the eyes of luxury customers.

However, if a maximum consistency initially represented the quintessence of impeccability, over time, it began to bring out some side effects, easing the need for the most sophisticated consumers to visit almost-identical flagship stores.

In this scenario, on the one hand, new local independent designers have started to emerge and gain visibility, thanks to the high value of craftsmanship and perceived uniqueness. On the other hand, new niche direct-to-consumer brands have started to flourish online, offering consumers the chance to get involved in a conversational environment and betting their best cards on conveying strong authenticity.

##### New Challenges *for Luxury Brands*

In this scenario, new challenges have, therefore, started to emerge for luxury brands willing to effectively enhance their customer engagement.

The first is probably to **rethink** the very role of physical points of sale, which are increasingly required to transform themselves into platforms for delivering unique, contextualized, and highly involving experiences, not necessarily aimed at conversions into immediate purchases to the extent that an increasingly important role is played today by digital platforms. Brands are required to fit into the lifestyle of their more and more local customers.

This trend has clearly been strongly accelerated by the pandemic, which has made virtual shop windows and video clienteling the only gateway to the brand world for several months and pushed new groups of consumers to experiment with online purchase processes.

In order to remain truly attractive and keep engaging customers who are expected to show increasingly nomadic behaviours across different channels, luxury brands are today required to further push on the accelerator of enhanced **phygital experiences** and **customized services**. Physical stores need to rediscover contextual routes to enhance experienced drivers that can even take them quite far away from their main value proposition but help literally immerse the consumer in the broader, and at the same time deeper, brand values.

We can just think about the many restaurants, hotels, and spas launched by many luxury brands before the pandemic, which has clearly put them on pause, but not under discussion in their attraction for future luxury consumption.

Enhanced customer experiences do not run just through redesigned environments, but also through differently conceived assortments. If luxury brands want to raise their engagement, they can also play with ad-hoc capsule collections designed in collaboration with local artists and exclusively provided in individual countries or even specific points of sale in order to allow us to truly rare pieces and signal a broader experience to the most sophisticated luxury consumers who are in search of new forms of conspicuous consumption, no longer related to economic affluence only.

In a constant search by luxury brands to find new ways to engage younger customers who are more and more used to digital catwalks, live shows and social networks, and even live stream shopping, **gamification** has also gained unprecedented diffusion to interact with consumers who are in the Millennials and Gen Z generation. They are expected, actually, to represent 50 percent of the luxury market in 2025, compared to 32 percent today.

Gamification hybridizes creativity with classic video gaming tools, *technology and fun*, *branding and virtual reality*. It can be a very attractive tool for new audiences, as has been proved by 12 million people watching the maxi avatar of Travis Scott performing an in-game gig for 10 minutes.

##### Luxury Brands' Interest *in Gamification*

Why are luxury brands so interested in this world, apparently so distant from their own exclusive DNA? The reasons are more than a few, but the common glue behind all of them is again customer engagement: in order to remain meaningful, luxury brands have to speak **the same language** of the tribes they want to target, as Louis Vuitton has understood by signing a partnership with Riot Games to develop five custom skins and a special case for the League of Legends trophy, plus several looks for Animal Crossing.

In this respect, gamification represents an innovative way to get in touch with younger targets and let them experience a truly immersive experience with the luxury brand by building storytelling in a more authentic way. It also provides a very effective opportunity to introduce and test new products on relevant audiences, playing with virtual and augmented reality to make the interaction more effective and get relevant insights to better fine-tune the new collections to be launched also in physical stores.

Not less relevant, gamification also offers a more pleasant and fun way to experience shopping.

The next frontier of omnichannel customer engagement probably comes from the recent developments of digital products and the so-called *non-fungible tokens (NFTs)*, unique digital assets whose authenticity and ownership is proved through the blockchain technology. The core idea is that when consumers buy a luxury item today, they will also get a digital representation of it that can be brought with them, for instance, into a video game.

##### Trend or *Real Opportunity*?

Is that just a temporary trend or a new real business opportunity for luxury brands? Once more, if the goal is to identify new ways to engage relevant younger audiences, providing them objects that can turn into extensions of themselves and help them communicate their identities might seem quite a business-savvy choice. And the more their lives turn online, the more their preferred objects have to follow them, until we reach the paradox of a brand that has recently sold a digital-only pair of shoes priced from $3,000 to $10,000 each, sending its buyers a physical pair of shoes as a thank-you note for their digital purchase.

In this increasingly seamless environment, interesting implications also arise from a customer knowledge perspective. The more consumers interact with brands, not only for buying a few items on a spot basis, but also for entering a more systematic conversation with them, the more they contribute to generating granular data, feeding an unprecedented goldmine of real-time behavioural information.

The so-called "*single view of the customer*", which develops across all the different touchpoints and activities consumers engage in with a specific brand, becomes an extraordinary opportunity to enhance the potential for customer relationship management and target consumers with phygital initiatives that are made to order on their own individual interests and preferences.

This is a further reason to invest in customer engagement today in order to maximize brand sales tomorrow.

#### Crafting successful brand experiences

**Anne Michaut**, Marketing Professor at **HEC Paris**, reveals the five critical success factors for creating memorable brand experiences. Learn how to transform ordinary interactions into extraordinary moments.

I am Anne Michaut, Marketing Professor at HEC Paris, and we are going to talk about experiences from the angle of success factors of experiences.

Creating memorable brand experiences is an intricate art that goes beyond traditional marketing strategies. Our exploration today focuses on five critical success factors that transform ordinary interactions into extraordinary moments.

Our framework encompasses five interconnected dimensions that serve as a foundation for exceptional brand experiences:

- Customized
- Relevant
- Involving
- Memorable
- Elevating

##### Customized: acknowledging individual value

Customization is about recognizing that each customer brings unique value to the brand. This goes far beyond categorizing customers based on financial metrics; it is about recognizing the unique way each individual interacts with and values the brand.

The goal is to *craft experiences* that feel *personally tailored*, making each customer feel uniquely *appreciated and understood*. This approach requires brands to develop a nuanced understanding of their customers that goes beyond surface-level demographics. It means creating segmented experiences that speak directly to specific customer groups, acknowledging their individual preferences, histories, and potential future interactions with the brand.

##### Relevant: celebrating brand heritage

Relevance is about deeply connecting brand experiences to their unique identity and historical narrative. This goes beyond simply referencing past achievements; it involves creating experiences that are authentically rooted in the brand's specific cultural and historical context.

By celebrating special moments and entertaining clients within their distinct brand universe, successful Maisons transform brand heritage from a *static historical record* into a *modern part* of the *customer experience*. Each interaction becomes an opportunity to tell the brand's story, to showcase its unique character, and to create meaningful connections that resonate with customers on a deeper level.

The key is to transform brand heritage from a historical footnote into a living, breathing part of the customer experience.

##### Involving: living the moment

The involving dimension represents a fundamental shift from passive consumption to active participation, which contributes to reinforcing emotional connection. This is not a unilateral process, but a dynamic, two-sided interaction where both the brand and the client play active roles in creating the experience.

Involvement means *creating moments* where customers are not mere recipients, but *co-creators* of their experience. This might involve interactive events, collaborative design processes, or engagement strategies that invite customers to become integral parts of the brand's narrative.

The most successful brands blur the lines between provider and consumer, creating experiences that feel like shared journeys rather than transactional exchanges.

##### Memorable: designing lasting impressions

Memorability stands at the heart of exceptional brand experiences. Inspired by Daniel Kahneman's research, which shows that memories are all we retain from our experiences, brands need to understand that customers base their *decisions* on *memories of experiences*, rather than the *experiences themselves*.

This requires a sophisticated understanding of human cognitive processes. Brands must design experiences with an awareness of psychological phenomena like the peak-end model, creating moments that are not just continuously pleasant but feature emotional peaks. These are experiences that become the stories customers share, the moments that are instantly shareable and deeply emotional.

As the remembering self is the true decision-maker, brands should also focus on crafting memories rather than experiences.

##### Elevating: beyond the ordinary

The elevating dimension challenges brands to transcend traditional transactional relationships. This is about creating experiences that expand customer perspectives, enrich their lives, and transport them beyond the ordinary.

Elevation may involve **educating** customers, **presenting products** in exceptional contexts, and **providing experiences** that are inherently **transformative**. It is not just about selling a product, but about offering a journey of discovery, learning, and personal growth. The most successful brands manage to make customers feel as though they have been introduced to something extraordinary, something that goes far beyond their initial expectations.

Truly exceptional brand experiences require **continuous reflection** and **strategic thinking**. Brands must constantly ask themselves **critical questions**: What are the unmissable components for all customers? What needs to be customized? How do we create proprietary moments? When and how do we involve our clients? Are we creating experiences that will become lifetime memories?

**Successful brand experiences** are not just about selling products, but about **creating moments** that resonate deeply with customers. By orchestrating experiences that are customized, relevant, involving, memorable, and elevating, brands can transform ordinary interactions into **extraordinary journeys of connection and meaning**.

Good morning. My name is Gautier Pigasse. I am the Head of Innovation and Blockchain within the LVMH Group IT team.

After an entrepreneurial endeavour and several roles within digital innovation in a startup environment and within LVMH, I joined the CEO of LVMH, Franck Le Moal, a year and a half ago to coordinate and set up the blockchain and NFTs projects for the Group and in support of our Maisons.

3. Providing *documentation* and use case catalogues.

5. Coordinating the Aura *Blockchain Consortium* that we founded a year ago.

##### What is the *Aura Blockchain Consortium*?

We created the Aura Blockchain Consortium to provide a standard for the luxury market and luxury industry. Blockchain is a distributed ledger technology used to *store and share* encrypted data through nodes operated by *network participants*. It is, in fact, a question of trust and transparency for luxury markets.

We wanted to provide our Maisons and clients all over the world with a safe environment to deal with common use cases and customer expectations. Right from the beginning, we knew that we had to work collaboratively with our competitors: OTB, Prada, Cartier, Richemont, and LVMH.

We all have the same use cases and the same information services that we use, for instance, in product lifecycle management and customer relationship management — sometimes the same tools or providers. But the data from upstream business, such as components, product lifecycle, distribution, and direct-to-consumer, is specific and unique to each Maison, and we want to secure it immutably.

By working together on a standard — basically a set of secure tools — we allow our Maisons to accelerate on key, common, and priority topics.

##### Why Use *the Blockchain*?

For us, it was a no-brainer. Blockchain is able to secure data immutably and permanently. This is why we decided to create an independent non-profit association in Geneva to provide this solution and certificate of authentication all over the world, acting as an authenticity label.

We *follow and secure* the whole product lifecycle from sourcing to shelf. We can do that thanks to *value-added services* that we provide to our clients, for instance: certificates of authentication, maintenance passports, and value-added content through a collectible — more commonly called an NFT.

Simply put, technically, it is a toolbox providing, on top of the blockchain, a set of APIs to connect and integrate to information services (product lifecycle management, enterprise resource planning, customer relationship management) and specific technologies such as authentication enablers (NFC, QR code, RFID), all direct-to-consumer for certificates and NFTs collectibles.

Ultimately, we create a seamless *luxury experience* in a *secure environment* to best meet our clients' expectations.

##### How Does *LVMH Use NFTs*?

In addition to the direct-to-consumer experience of providing value-added services, we also have several projects combining physical product traceability and value-added content, thanks to blockchain technologies and NFTs.

An NFT is a non-fungible token, meaning that it is a unique *token* on the blockchain that belongs to someone and stores specific data content. Because this token is unique, it cannot be exchanged for another one. For cryptocurrencies, for instance, one Bitcoin token has the same value as another Bitcoin token at the same time and therefore can be exchanged — which is the opposite of an NFT.

The technology is the same for NFTs on private blockchains and public blockchains. This means that we can provide value-added content linked to a physical product, such as a digital twin (a unique asset, for example, 3D modelling), a collectible for designer or Maison lovers, or a proof of attendance at an event, and so on.

##### Any Exciting Projects *within the LVMH Maisons*?

We have had a few projects and have several ongoing projects with some Maisons.

For instance, if you buy a product, you can benefit from an NFT as a digital twin, which will bring value-added content to that physical product. We have had several projects with Hublot, Givenchy, and Bvlgari.

A second use case: you buy an NFT and you will benefit from a product after a certain time. So we are dealing with after-sales and pre-delivery use cases, for instance, in the wines and spirits industry or for special orders within the jewellery industry.

A third use case is fully digital: for example, proof of attendance, or if you simply want to buy an NFT, as we have done with Givenchy and the designer Chito.

##### What is *Next*?

We want to provide a seamless luxury user experience. This means that we need to ensure that our clients who are going to buy a product in a store can benefit instantly — within one second — from an NFT on their mobile phone, as proof of authenticity, for trust, or whatever other purpose.
